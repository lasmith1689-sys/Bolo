# Bolo

Everyday Gujarati for iPhone, taught as a game. **બોલો** (bolo) means "speak".

Each new phrase arrives as a short illustrated conversation between two people, with the phrase
outlined in its speech bubble. Every Gujarati word shows its romanization underneath; tap it and its
English meaning appears above it. Then the phrase is quizzed, and a session becomes a **printing
run**: right answers stamp blocks onto a strip of cloth, three in a row print gold, five in a row is
"double ink", and a session with no gaps is a clean panel framed in gold.

The Gujarati script is always shown and never tested. Romanization is the working surface.

## What's in it

- **56 phrases in 7 units** (first words, meeting people, getting by, numbers, food and drink,
  people, out and about), each taught in one of **26 two-person scenes**.
- **Tap-to-gloss** on every word, from one global 94-word dictionary (so છે reads "is" everywhere),
  and romanization under every word.
- **Five exercises:** teach (the conversation), recognise (Gujarati to English), recall (English to
  Gujarati), listen (audio to English), and assemble (tap the romanized words into order).
- **Leitner spaced repetition:** five boxes with intervals of 0, 1, 3, 7 and 21 days. A session is
  up to five new phrases (each taught, then quizzed) plus the due reviews, at most 14 graded cards to
  start with; misses go back to the end of the session.
- **Scoring:** 10 XP a right answer, 15 from three in a row, 25 from five in a row. Daily streak and
  personal-best run. A miss offers **See it in conversation**, so the phrase is met again in context.
- **Units as printing blocks** that fill with madder dye as their phrases reach box 3.
- **Audio, two ways, to compare in Settings:**
  - *Recorded (AI):* every line pre-recorded by an open neural text-to-speech model, with a different
    voice for each of the two people. Bundled in the app, so it works offline.
  - *iPhone voice:* the best Gujarati system voice installed on the phone (premium, then enhanced,
    then default), read at a learner-friendly pace.

  Lines play with the silent switch on. A *Slower playback* switch slows both.

## Design

The visual language is **Ajrakh** block printing from Kutch, carried over from the original
prototype: an indigo ground, madder for action and stamps, pomegranate gold for runs and
romanization, over-dyed green only for "correct". Display type is **Eczar** (drawn for Indic
scripts) and all Gujarati and body text is **Hind Vadodara** (named for a Gujarat city). Every card is
framed top and bottom by a diamond-and-dot border band, and a gold rule over the answer (the
shirorekha, the top bar Gujarati dropped from Devanagari) retracts when the answer is revealed. The
38 illustrations are single-weight line drawings inside an octagonal printing block; the numbers
unit shows the Gujarati numeral instead.

## How it's built

There is no Mac in this project: GitHub Actions does all the building.

| Workflow | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **CI** | every push to `main` | BoloKit unit tests on macOS; Simulator build, launch and a 10-screen smoke test with screenshots (also pushed to the `ci/screenshots` branch); App Store archive dry run with an entitlements check |
| **TestFlight** | by hand, or a commit message containing `[ship]` | signs with Apple's cloud signing and uploads to TestFlight (see [TESTFLIGHT.md](TESTFLIGHT.md)) |
| **Audio** | `content.json` changes, or by hand | records missing or stale clips, verifies them and commits them |
| **TTS probe** | research only | compares candidate Gujarati TTS models; results on the `probe/*` branches |

The Xcode project is generated by [XcodeGen](https://github.com/yonaskolb/XcodeGen) from
`project.yml` and isn't committed.

```
App/                    SwiftUI app: views, design system, audio, demo states for screenshots
  Resources/            fonts (with their OFL licences), asset catalog (icon, motifs), Audio/
Packages/BoloKit/       the game's rules as a Swift package, with unit tests
  Sources/BoloKit/Resources/content.json   phrases, scenes, gloss and romanization
Config/                 Info.plist and build settings
tools/                  scripts that made the fonts, motifs, icon and audio
.github/                workflows and their scripts
```

To change the content, edit `content.json` and push: the tests check that every phrase appears in
its scene and every word has a meaning and a romanization, and the Audio workflow records any new
lines.

## Audio: how the recordings are made

`tools/tts/synthesize.py`, run by the Audio workflow on Ubuntu runners:

1. Works out every clip the app can ask for: each scene line in its speaker's voice (Luke male,
   Meera female), plus each phrase on its own. That is 95 clips.
2. Synthesizes them with **[Svara TTS v1](https://huggingface.co/kenpath/svara-tts-v1)** (Apache
   2.0), a speech model for Indian languages with male and female Gujarati voices. It samples its
   output, so each line is drawn up to five times and the take that Meta's MMS speech recognizer
   transcribes most accurately (at a plausible length) is kept.
3. Trims silence, normalises loudness, encodes mono AAC, and fails loudly if any clip is missing or
   near-silent.

The model was chosen by the TTS probe (`probe/*` branches): Svara scored highest on an automatic
naturalness estimate (UTMOS), AI4Bharat Indic-TTS was more reliable but flatter, Meta MMS-TTS has a
single voice and a non-commercial licence, and AI4Bharat's Indic Parler-TTS is gated on Hugging
Face. The earlier espeak and MBROLA voices were dropped for good. See `tools/tts/` for details.
